# -payment-experience-ideas-simple-and-product-focused
Exploratory product concepts for improving digital payment convenience, transaction organisation, subscription tracking, and relevant customer communication.



Digital Payment Feature Ideas

Overview

This project presents a set of product ideas aimed at making digital payments easier to complete, easier to organise, and more useful to customers.

The concepts focus on four areas:

1. Scan-to-Pay
2. Payment Description & Categorisation
3. Subscription Identification & Tracking
4. Relevant News and Offers via Email

These concepts are presented as independent product ideas and exploratory proposals.

⸻

1. Scan-to-Pay

Typing an account number manually can be inconvenient and may lead to errors.

The Scan-to-Pay concept allows a customer to use their phone camera to capture an account number that is clearly visible instead of manually typing every digit.

The account number could be captured from:

* a clearly printed business account number;
* a picture containing the account number;
* a receipt or invoice;
* a POS display;
* a payment sign; or
* another clearly displayed source.

For example, if a business has its bank account number clearly printed at a payment point, the customer could simply scan it.

Proposed Flow

Scan → Detect Account Number → Verify Recipient → Confirm → Pay

The camera captures the displayed account number and image/text recognition converts it into editable text.

The account details would then go through the normal account verification process so that the customer can see and confirm the recipient before transferring money.

Scanning is therefore intended to reduce manual typing, not replace normal payment verification.

⸻

2. Payment Description & Categorisation

After making or preparing a payment, customers could quickly describe what the transaction was for by selecting a category.

For example:

Payment to ABC Supermarket

What was this payment for?

* Groceries
* Transport
* Food
* Shopping
* Education
* Health
* Bills
* Entertainment
* Subscription
* Other

If the customer selects Groceries, that transaction would be recorded as a grocery expense.

This creates a simple way for customers to organise their transactions without having to type descriptions manually.

Example

₦25,000 → ABC Supermarket → Groceries

Instead of seeing only the merchant or recipient later, the customer can immediately understand what the money was spent on.

⸻

3. Subscription Identification & Tracking

Customers often pay for recurring services using their cards.

When a card is used for a service that may be recurring, the app could provide an option such as:

“Is this a subscription?”

If the customer selects Yes, the payment would be added to their subscription tracker.

For example:

Streaming Service — ₦7,000

Is this a subscription?

Yes | No

Once confirmed as a subscription, the app could automatically begin tracking the subscription from that payment.

The tracker could show:

* subscription name;
* amount paid;
* date of the last payment;
* estimated next payment date;
* number of active subscriptions; and
* estimated total subscription spending.

Example

My Subscriptions

Netflix — ₦7,000/month
Spotify — ₦1,300/month
iCloud — ₦1,500/month

Estimated Monthly Subscriptions: ₦9,800

This would help customers recognise recurring expenses and understand how much of their money is committed to subscriptions.

The customer should also be able to remove an incorrectly identified subscription or stop tracking it.

⸻

4. Relevant News & Offers via Email

Instead of placing another news or promotional section inside the application, useful information and relevant offers could be sent through email.

The goal would not be to send every promotion to every customer.

Where the customer has given appropriate consent, the information sent could be related to the categories of products or services they normally spend money on.

For example, a customer who frequently categorises transactions as Groceries could receive relevant grocery-related offers or information.

A customer who spends on Entertainment or Subscriptions could receive relevant information relating to those categories.

This creates a simple flow:

Customer Spending Category → Relevant Information → Email

Customers would therefore receive fewer unrelated messages while having a better chance of seeing information that is useful to them.

Customers should also have control over whether they receive these emails and be able to unsubscribe.

⸻

How the Ideas Connect

The concepts can work independently but can also complement one another.

Scan

Capture a clearly displayed account number.

↓

Verify

Confirm the account and recipient.

↓

Describe

Select what the payment was for, such as Groceries.

↓

Identify

If appropriate, indicate whether the payment represents a subscription.

↓

Track

Keep a record of recurring subscription payments.

↓

Inform

With the customer’s permission, send relevant news or offers through email based on appropriate spending categories.

⸻

Why These Ideas?

Convenience

Scanning can reduce the effort required to enter account numbers manually.

Accuracy

Account verification remains part of the payment process even when an account number is captured from an image.

Organisation

Simple descriptions such as Groceries, Transport, or Education can make transaction histories easier to understand.

Subscription Awareness

Allowing customers to identify card payments as subscriptions can make recurring expenses easier to track.

Relevance

Optional category-based email communication can help customers receive information and offers that are more relevant to them.

⸻

Important Considerations

Any implementation of these concepts should prioritise:

* recipient verification before transfers;
* customer confirmation of scanned information;
* manual-entry alternatives;
* secure handling of payment information;
* customer control over transaction categories;
* customer control over subscription identification;
* privacy and consent for personalised communication; and
* easy opt-out from promotional emails.
